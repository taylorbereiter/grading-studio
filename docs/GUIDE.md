# Grading Studio: the full guide

Everything the app does, screen by screen. For what it is and how to start, see the [README](../README.md).

Where this guide says ManageBac or Atlas, read the name of your own school's system: the app uses whichever name you gave it in the welcome (or in Settings, under Classes and rosters), and plain words if you gave none.

## How to use
1. Download the class's submissions from your school's system (ManageBac, Google Classroom, Canvas, Teams, Moodle…). Drop the ZIP (or the unzipped folder, or the files) onto the page.

   **Getting around.** The sidebar on the left is always there: **Find anything** (Ctrl K, or ⌘K), **Home**, **Planner**, each class by name, and **Settings**. Home has the work to pick up and the box to open a download, and, once the Planner knows your timetable, **Today’s classes**: each class meeting today with its time and the lesson planned for it (click one to open that lesson, or **Plan it**). Each class has its own page with its units and assignments. **Settings** is one page per subject, each with its own address: Saving and carrying, Classes and rosters, Terms and weights, Grading scale and rubrics, Marking, Timetable and calendar, Display, Working with Claude, and Keys. While a paper is open the sidebar folds to icons. Every screen has its own address, so Refresh comes back to the same paper, and Back and Forward work.
2. An open assignment has four tabs in the top bar: **Mark** (the papers), **Grades**, **Insights** and **Return**. Each one is a page with its own address, so Refresh, Back and Forward work, and Escape or **Mark** goes back to the papers. **Setup** beside them holds what you set once for the assignment: its roster, Rubric setup, Bring in files, What opened, Transcripts, Quick mark, Marks from other apps and the Atlas link. The class name before the assignment's name goes to that class's page, with its Gradebook. Everything else (Lighting, Rescans, Display, Mark key, Calculator, Dark mode, All keys) is in **Find anything** (Ctrl K) and in Settings. The ✕ goes back to Home.
3. Students are listed in folder order, or in roster order once a roster is picked. The tools are a strip down the left edge of the paper: Select, Pen, Highlight (it snaps to lines of typed text, and goes freehand on scans), Circle, Text, Comment (a box in the margin with a line to the spot), Check and Eraser, then Undo, Redo and Zoom. Point at a tool for its name and its key; the tool you're on shows its choices (ink, width, Snap or Free) under it. Use ← → to move between students.
   **The student list.** While you mark, the list is a narrow strip down the left: each student's number and how far their score is (the total, a ring for part-scored, a dot for none). Point at it and the names open over the paper; move away and it folds. Press **[** (or the round button at its foot) to keep it open beside the paper, and again to fold it; that choice is remembered. **/** opens it with the keyboard in Find. Above the names: how many are scored, the average (click it for Insights), and one filter button with **To score**, **Flagged** (a page to rescan, a grade on the paper to confirm, a score that differs from the paper, a new or changed file), and **No paper**, **Late** and **Left out** when there are any. A filter shows just those papers plus the one you're on, and Previous, Next and ← → walk the list as it shows; click the filter again for everyone. It lasts for this visit only. At the foot of the list: **Add late work** (Bring in files) and **Hide names** (anonymous grading).
   **Menus.** **+ Add** above the paper adds a file, pasted text or pages from a scan to this student. The **⋯** beside it has Arrange pages, Lighting and the Calculator. Each file's own **⋯** has Turn all pages, Group work…, Move to…, Replace…, Leave out and Remove (and Download on a Word or other file); a Word paper keeps Comment, Edit and Convert to PDF on its bar. Escape closes a menu, and ↑ ↓ move in it.
4. Score with the rubric on the right: a slider per row, 4 to 0 with half points (drag it, click a number under it, or use the arrow keys; Delete clears the row). 3 rows = out of 12 with your grading scale (10.5 = 93%). **Rubric setup** (under Setup, or Edit above the rubric) renames rows, says what each score means on a row, saves a rubric to reuse, or switches to plain points. Taking a row out keeps every other score on its own row. If a change would take scores away or stop them counting (a row with scores on it, or switching between rows and a total), Save asks first; scores that stop counting stay saved and come back if you switch back. Under the total, **Mark as** sets **Missing**, **Excused** or **Late**. Missing and excused have no score and aren't counted as still to do; late keeps its score. Nothing is ever taken off for them. Giving a missing or excused student a score takes the mark off. The **⋯** beside them has **Leave out of the average**, which keeps a paper's score (it still copies) but leaves it out of the average, Class overview and Consistency check: for a one-off case, a placeholder 0, or a paper graded somewhere else.
5. **Grades** tab: every student in folder order or roster order, whichever you choose, with row scores, total, percent, status and your ManageBac comment. Click any cell to copy it. **On the paper** shows the grade written on each paper beside your score; where the two differ, or a paper has more than one grade or a doubtful one, a line at the top says how many to check, with **Show only these**. A rubric with only some rows scored says **partial** here, in the student list, in copies and in the spreadsheet, and copying one asks you first. The spreadsheet is safe to open: a name or comment that starts like a formula comes through as plain text.
6. **Grade sheet** (the second view of the Grades tab): for papers you marked here or in Kami, it finds a grade you typed (like "9.5 = 88%", "9.5/12 = 88%" or "10/12") and shows a cropped picture of it per file, with the folder and file name on each row. Click a picture to open that exact file. If a paper has two or more things that look like grades, anywhere on it, in one file or several, all of them are shown and flagged "check", none of them counts, and each one asks **Is this the grade?** until you pick one with **Use it**. A number with a label or points beside it but no slash or percent, like "Revised: 10" or "10 points", is always asked about too. A grade worked out once in one place ("9.5/12 = 88%") is one grade, and a plain count ("3/12 sources cited") isn't one. Dates and page numbers ("Due 10/12", "page 3/12") aren't grades. Anything doubtful, like "10/15" on a 12-point assignment, a fraction inside a longer comment, or two grades in one comment, asks **Is this the grade?** and doesn't count until you press **Use it**. So does anything a student could have typed: a grade in the page's own text (a flattened Kami grade looks the same), a line in a Word file, or shaded boxes in a Word table, your own rubric in a student's file included. Your own marks and Kami notes in the forms above count by themselves, and when you delete or change the mark, the grade goes with it. A confirmed "10/15" says "10 out of 15" wherever it shows and copies. Grades written by hand? Use **Pick the spot by hand** and drag a box where the grade goes; that spot is cropped on every paper. Once a spot is picked, only that spot is read, so a second grade outside it isn’t noticed. Letter grades like B+ aren’t read.
7. **Insights** tab: the class average, grade bands, a dot for every paper, the spread of scores, the rubric rows the class found hardest, how many papers got each score on each row, each student's change since the last summative, the same assignment in your other classes (when it was given to several), the class across assignments, and who's missing or unfinished. Its **Percents** view is yours to slice, and **Consistency** is the Consistency check. It says who it left out and why. A score of 0 counts. Missing and excused never count as 0, and a partial total stays out of the spread, though its scored rows count in the row averages. A grade read from the paper that isn't on this assignment's scale (10/15 on a 12-point rubric, or just a percent) is left out and named. Late scores count. During anonymous grading it shows paper numbers only.
   **Gradebook** (the Gradebook tab on a class's page; while grading, the class name in the top bar goes there): every assignment for a class in one table, grouped by type, with an average for each type and a term grade. Each assignment has a type (Summative assessment, In-class assessment or Formative activity), set in New / Edit assignment, from its column, or all at once with Set types for older ones. **Term setup** holds your own terms (an assignment counts toward the term its due date falls in) and how much each type weighs; inside a type, every assignment counts the same. Formative activities show a ✓ when they have feedback and don't count unless you give them weight. **+ Add scores** types or pastes scores for work you did before Grading Studio, as points or percents; it becomes an assignment card tagged Typed in. Copy table copies it for a spreadsheet. It all reads saved work in this browser and stays there.
   **Charts** (the Charts tab on a class's page) draws the same numbers: every student against every assignment in one grid of blues, stronger for a higher score, sortable by any heading; who is moving (each student's last three scores against their earlier ones); how many students landed in each range on each assignment; the class average on each rubric row across the summatives; each student's summative average against their in-class average; and term grade bands, each assignment's class average over time, the spread on each assignment and your classes side by side. Point at any mark to see what it is. **One student** (click a name in the Students tab, the gradebook or the grid) shows their term as a strip of every assignment, their scores as a line over the middle half of the class, and their rubric rows against the class average (← → to move). A student's page is addressed by their place on the roster, so no name goes into the browser's history.
8. **Return** tab: starts with a check of the class: scores not finished, students with no paper, scores that disagree with the paper, files with no marks and grades to confirm. Click a name to go there; you can still export. It reads and crops the grades first, then flattens every marked file (your marks here and any Kami marks) into a ZIP with the same student folders, plus the grade sheet PDF. Files with no marks are left out. A paper with proofreading marks gets a key to them as its last page (see Mark key below). Your original files are never changed. Once the files have gone out (ZIP, folder or Drive), the assignment says **Returned** on Home and on its class page; **Mark as not returned** under the button takes that back. Beside it: **One student at a time** (one student's marked paper now, and who has had theirs), and **Ask Claude**, with the steps below. One gap: a copy of your own Word rubric from Rubric setup doesn’t go in the ZIP, even when it’s scored and even though the box says Word rubrics come back, so use **Download** on that copy. A Word rubric in the student’s own folder does come back.
   **Just one student**: **Export [name]’s paper now**, under Previous and Next, downloads only that student’s marked file the moment you finish them, named for them (a ZIP if they have more than one), with the same rules and your saved Options. They get a green **sent** tag in the student list.

9. **Remove** and **Replace…** are under the ⋯ on every file's bar, including files that came with the download. Remove asks for a second click, and first says how many marks are on the file if it has any; a note with **Undo** follows. Replace… picks a new file (a rescan or a corrected copy), shows the old and new side by side, and puts the new one in the old one's place. Nothing is thrown away: removed and replaced files wait under **Removed files** at the bottom of that student's paper with their marks, and **Bring back** puts them back. They stay out of the export, and stay removed when the download is opened again. To take out one page, point at it and press **×** in its top corner (beside ⟲ ⟳ ⚑). That works on your rubric page too, and on the old rubric pages inside a full PDF you brought in. The page waits under **Removed pages** with its marks and **Bring back**, which puts it back in its place. Arrange pages starts with those pages in its Taken out tray.
10. **Arrange pages** (the ⋯ beside the student's name) combines a student's files and drags pages into order; extra papers with no marks are left out by default (Bring back restores them).
11. **Share with Claude** (Return tab, under Ask Claude): makes a ZIP with no names. Each student gets a random code (S-XXXX); the key stays in your browser. Every page becomes a picture. Typed names, the student's and classmates', with or without accents, are covered in black, along with anything typed in a form box that has a name, email addresses and long ID numbers. Names in your comments become [name], as they’re spelled on the folder, so a name with a changed ending (Андрею for Андрей) or a one-character given name on its own stays as typed; read over comments written in those languages. Automatic name-finding will never catch everything, so each paper is looked over too: if its own student's name can't be found on any page and there's anything on the paper at all (typed text, a form box, a picture, even strokes drawn on a tablet), it's marked **Name not found, check this one**. If the name is in another alphabet and was found and covered, it's marked **Name in another alphabet, check this one**, since a PDF's text can scramble or lose those letters (Hindi's order, Thai's stacked marks). The box lists those papers and sends you to Check the covers before you make the ZIP, and anonymous grading does the same before you start. Any part of the name counts as found, even in small letters, so Ben Hall's paper saying "down the hall" isn't marked. Word and text papers can't be boxed, so before anonymous grading starts, any with no sign of the student's name are listed and say so on the paper, since a nickname or initials would still show. To cover a name on one, save it as a PDF and add that instead. An everyday word that's also someone's name, like My or Will, only counts when it's written like a name, and a classmate's name at the start of a sentence stays as typed, since "Will you" looks the same; the box says so when a class has names like these, so look for them on the papers and in your comments. Names that are rarely anything else, like Ruby or Grace, count there too, and in capitals. The same goes for a name in any alphabet that comes down to an everyday word: with a classmate named علي, the word على stays as typed, and it only counts on his own paper or as part of his full name. Only PDFs and pictures go in: the box says plainly who is left out, such as a student with only a Word file, and their scores and comments stay out too. The box lists the steps in order. **Check the covers** walks paper by paper so you can box handwriting it missed ("Use my last box on every paper" for a fixed name spot). Press **Covers look right** on each one: the box then shows which papers are checked and which aren't, the check starts at the first one not done, and changing a cover or a file, or turning a page, means another look. Your boxes turn with the page. You can still make the ZIP with some unchecked; its button says so. Then **Copy the message** gives you something to paste to Claude with the ZIP, and **Open Claude** opens a new tab. Nothing goes anywhere until you carry it there. The ZIP holds papers/S-XXXX.pdf, scores.csv, comments.json, rubric.json and a README telling Claude how to answer.
   **Marks only**, the second choice at the top of Share with Claude, makes one Markdown file instead of the ZIP, for asking Claude about patterns across the class. It has no papers in it, so there are no covers to check. Students get the same codes as the ZIP. It holds whichever of these you tick: rubric scores, totals, percents and status (with what each score means, if you wrote it); your comment box, margin comments, text boxes and Kami comments, by page; each proofreading mark and sticker, with a count per student and for the class; the typed words under your highlights, circles, comments and proofreading marks; and a count of pen marks and checks per page. Names in your text and in the quoted words become [name], and emails and long ID numbers are taken out. Words are only quoted from a paper's typed text, never from handwriting or scans. Students with no marks, score or comment are left out, and the file says how many.
12. **Bring back from Claude** (Return tab, under Ask Claude): paste Claude's reply (blocks starting with a code) or drop its CSV/ZIP. It shows code, student and feedback for you to read, then puts each one in that student's comment box. It tells you who the reply left out, with a note you can copy asking Claude for those, and any reply that still says [name]. Pasting the same reply twice doesn’t double it. PDFs named by code can be downloaded sorted into student folders.

## Handy extras
Shortcuts below are written the Mac way. On Windows, the app and this list mean Ctrl for ⌘ and Ctrl+Shift for ⇧⌘, and the app's own labels say so.

- **Turn a page**: point at a page and use ⟲ ⟳ in its top corner, or **⟳ Turn all pages** under a file's ⋯. In Arrange pages, pick pages and press R (or ⟳ Turn). Your marks turn with the page, the turn is kept, and the export comes out turned.
- **Paper scans**: when a scanned page opens, or as a scan is read for splitting, the app looks at the ink. If it's sure a page is upside down, on its side or crooked, it fixes it and says so above the page (or on the page in the split), with **Put back**. If it isn't sure, it asks. It also says when a page looks blank or faint, and **Fix the lighting** does it for that student. Blank pages are never removed unless you press **Leave out blank pages**, which first shows you each page it would take out, drawn darker so faint writing shows up, with **Keep this page** on each. Nothing goes until you press the button in that view, and any page can be brought back. A page with your marks on it is only ever asked about; if it changes, the marks go with it. Math and code pages are never turned by themselves, since short lines can fool the look at the ink: one that seems to be on its side asks, with **Turn left** and **Turn right**. A faint page of writing is called faint, never blank, so a very pale blank back with print showing through it may be called faint too. A page with nothing but a name in the top margin can still be called blank, which is one reason you see the pages first. Pages with typed text or Kami notes are left alone. A scan with its words read by OCR or a copier is still turned and flagged, but not straightened, so its words stay under the page. A straightened page can be put back later with ⟂ in its top corner. The export comes out the way the page looks.
- **Undo and redo**: ⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z (or ⌘Y), also in the tool strip.
- **Zoom**: pinch or ⌘-scroll on the paper, ⌘+ / ⌘− / ⌘0, or click the percent to go back to 100%.
- **Picked marks** (Select tool): arrow keys nudge (Shift for bigger steps), ⌘C / ⌘V copies a mark to the page you're looking at (even another student's), ⌘D duplicates, Delete removes, Esc lets go.
- **Everyone on the roster**: with a class picked, every roster student is in the list, even with nothing turned in yet (marked "no paper"). Click their name for **Add their file…** or **From a scan…**.
- **Work sent in an email**: **+ Add → Pasted text…** (or **Paste text…** on a student with nothing turned in) takes text a student sent in an email or a message. Name it, paste it, and it's added to that student as a PDF page after their other files, to mark, score and export like any paper, with Replace… and Remove. Typed words stay real text, so highlights snap to them. Text the page's font can't write (Thai, Russian, emoji) comes in as a picture of the page instead, so pen, highlighter and comments still work but highlights don't snap, and Share with Claude can't cover a name in it, so check those covers by hand.
- **Late work**: **Bring in files** (under Setup) takes the late files, or the whole new download: anything already here is left out, so only what's new is left to place. Tick **Late** beside anyone you want marked late; nobody is marked late unless you say so. **New student…** adds someone who wasn't in the download. **+ Add → A file…** or a drop on a name does one student, and **Mark late** on the file does the marking. Opening a newer download from Home or its class page carries your work over and offers to mark students with new files late.
- **What opened**: after a download opens, a bar at the bottom of the paper says how many files opened, how many didn't, who has nothing, and what looks odd (the same file from two students, someone not on the roster, a file named for another student). **See the list** has the details and what to do about each one. When everything is fine nothing is said, and once you've seen it, it stays quiet until something new turns up. **What opened** under Setup shows it any time. During anonymous grading it says Paper 3, with no names or file names.
- **A makeup scan**: with a download open, **Bring in files → A scan to split…** splits another scan into it. Drag pages onto names like the first split; students already in the download get the pages as an extra file, and roster students who weren't in it yet are added in roster order.
- **Group work**: for a poster or any paper several students made together, pick **Group work…** under the file's ⋯ and click each partner. Each partner gets their own copy of the file, so the export gives each of them the marked paper. With **Mark and grade once for the group** on (the default), a mark made on any copy is on all of them, and a score, comment or status given to any partner is everyone's. A blue band above the paper names the group; **Grade on their own…** there gives one student their own grade and their own copy of the marks so far, and the rest stay together. The list shows a **group** chip. In a scan split, turn on **Give to several**, pick the pages and click each student's name; each student gets those pages, and the band on their paper offers **Mark and grade once for the group**. Marks are shared when every page of the file went to all of them; otherwise only the grade is. Remove on a copy takes it off that student.
- **Hard-to-read scans in the split**: **☀ Lighting** in the split has brightness, contrast, darken writing, sharpen and black and white, for the whole scan or the picked pages. It changes the pages there and in the big view, never the scan. With **Keep it for grading** ticked (the default), the students who get those pages open with the same lighting: lighting for the whole scan becomes the download's, and lighting on picked pages goes to just those pages of each student who got them. Black and white is in the Lighting panel while grading too.
- **Rescans**: pick pages in the split and press **⚑ Needs rescan**. They can still be given to a student. Once open, the page says it needs a rescan, the student shows ⚑ in the list, the **Flagged** filter above the list shows just those papers, and **Rescans** (on the Return tab when any are waiting, or in Find anything) lists everyone waiting, with **Copy the list**. ⚑ in a page's top corner flags or unflags a page while grading. When the rescan comes in, add it to the student (**Bring in the rescan…** on the page does the same as + Add → A file…) or split it in with Bring in files, then press **It's fine now** on the old page.
- **Change marks after exporting**: with **Keep marks editable** ticked (the default), each exported PDF also carries the original pages and your marks. Open the exported ZIP or folder like any download, or add one exported file to a student, and the marks come back as marks you can move, change or delete. The files are about twice as big. Marks baked in by an older export can't be taken apart.
- **Lighting** (the ⋯ beside the student's name, Find anything, or the ☀ chip on a page): brightness, contrast, darken writing and sharpen (for fuzzy scans), for all students or just one. Auto fix keeps your sharpen setting. Each page has a ☀ chip in its corner: click it to turn lighting off or on for that page. Its ▾ opens the same sliders for that page alone, with **Auto fix this page**, **Same as the rest**, and a way to use that page's lighting on the rest of the file or the same page on every paper. A small dot on the chip marks a page with its own lighting. Turning the page off and on again keeps it.
- **Readings** (button above each paper): files shown above the papers while you grade, never sent back. Add one for everyone, untick it for one student, or add a file just for that student (a different reading for a makeup). "Hide for this student" on a reading does the same in one click.
- **Saved comments**: under the comment box in the score panel, your first three saved comments are one click each, and **Saved comments** has the rest, with **Save this comment** for what you've just typed. A click adds the comment after whatever is already in the box. They're the same saved comments the Comment tool offers on the paper.
- **Scoring from the keyboard**: the rubric row in hand is tinted. Press **1** to **4** (or **0**) to score it; the next row is then in hand. A **full stop** right after adds or takes off a half. **Enter** opens the next paper to score, so a class can be scored without the mouse. On a paper where every row has a score, a number changes nothing until you click a row or press **Tab** to it, so a stray key can't undo a score. With plain points, a number starts typing in the score box. Numbers typed in a comment, in Find or with the calculator open are never scores.
- **Next to score**: while any paper has no full score, the big button under the comment is **Next to score** (Enter, or N), with ‹ and › beside it for the paper before and after. Once everyone has a score it's plain **Next**.
- **Straight lines**: hold Shift while drawing with the pen or a freehand highlight.
- **Highlighting**: drag along typed text and it snaps to whole words, across as many lines as you drag, as one mark. Start away from any text, or on a page with no text, and it's freehand. Press H again (or use Snap / Free in the tool strip) to switch; the S, M or L button sets how wide a freehand highlight is. Dragging the Comment tool over typed words highlights them the same way.
- **Right-click on a paper** for almost any tool, used once, so the tool you're on (Comment, say) stays picked. **Add a comment here** is first, then a check mark and your recent picks. On a typed word, **Circle** and **Highlight** do that word in one click; anywhere, **Draw a circle** or **Draw a highlight** lets you drag one, then goes back to your tool (Esc cancels). **Text box here** opens a box to type in. **Write with the pen** stays on until you press **Done**, Esc or right-click again. **Punctuation** (period, comma, apostrophe, quotes, semicolon, colon, question mark, exclamation, hyphen, dash), **Proofreading marks** (circled gr for grammar, capitalize, lowercase, insert, new paragraph, delete, swap, space, close up, and circled sp, RO, frag, WC, awk, VT, ?) and **Stickers** open in a panel beside the menu when you point at them. **Ink** at the bottom sets the color. Right-click one of your marks for **Delete this mark** (and **Edit** on a comment or text box). A mark lands where you clicked, in your ink color; stickers keep their own colors. Each one is a normal mark: move, copy, erase, undo, and it goes in the export. Hover over one to see what it means.
- **Quick mark (paper quiz)**: box each question once on any scan, then mark one question for the whole class or one student's whole quiz. Boxes can overlap; drag a box's number to move it, a corner to resize it. Each question picks how it's marked on the paper: **✗ on wrong** (the default; right answers get nothing), **✓ and ✗**, or **Points** ("1/2"), with half points from **+½** or H. Each question's mark goes in the margin or at a spot you pick, and dragging a mark on one answer moves it on that paper only (**Reset spot** puts it back). **Size** (S, M, L, Full width) sets how big answers show and is remembered on each computer. **Not marked** hides what's done, **⤢ Whole page** (or Space) shows the full page, and Ctrl Z takes back the last mark.
- **Calculator** (the = key, the ⋯ beside the student's name or beside Missing, Excused, Late, or Find anything): type the numbers straight in. The = key on its own adds; Shift = or Enter gives the answer. **Use as score** puts it in the score box; **Score sticker** does that too, then you click where a boxed score and percent goes on the paper. The Grade sheet reads that sticker as the paper's grade, so there's nothing to box by hand.
- **Mark key**: each returned PDF with proofreading or punctuation marks on it gets one more page at the end, listing only the marks used on that paper, each drawn as it looks on the page, with what it means. Stickers and check marks aren't listed. **Mark key** (in Settings, in Find anything, or from Options on the Return tab) has every mark with its name and meaning in plain words to start with; change any of them, leave a meaning blank to show just the name, or use **Back to the usual wording**. The name you give a mark is also what the right-click menu shows. Turn the key off there or under Options on the Return tab. Word files go back as Word files, so they get no key, and an editable export brought back in leaves the key page out; the next export makes a new one.
- **Marks from other apps**: a PDF that comes in already marked in Kami, Acrobat or Preview shows those marks, but you can't change them, and its comments sit read-only in the margin. A note above the paper says how many there are, with **Make them editable** and **Not now**. Making them editable turns each one into the matching mark here: pen and lines into your pen, highlights into your highlighter (a highlight with a comment into your highlight comment), typed text into your text, comments into your margin comments, and circles and boxes into your oval. Then you can move, change or erase them like your own, and a grade written in one still counts. Stamps, pictures and attached files stay on the page as they were, and so does everything in a locked file. **Put back** in the same note takes your copies away and shows the file's own marks again. **Marks from other apps** under Setup does every paper in the download at once, after showing what it found, and can do it by itself for each paper you open from then on. Your original files are never changed.
- **Resize** a picked sticker, mark, check, circle or text box by dragging the square at its corner, or press + and −. The next one of that kind comes out at the size you last gave it, until you open a different download.
- **Click a mark with any tool.** A click on a sticker, mark, check or circle picks it up (Select) so you can move or resize it; a click on one of your text boxes opens it for typing. Pen and highlights still draw over everything.
- **Clicking off an open comment** just closes it (saving what you typed). The next click starts a new one.
- **Highlighting over a highlight** swaps the color: the same color again looks the same, and a new color covers the old one instead of mixing.
- **Comments on words** look different from a plain highlight: a lighter tint, a line under the words in the comment's color, and a number that matches the comment's card in the margin.
- **Text boxes**: click for a box, or drag across to set its width. With the Text tool, drag a box to move it and click it to type. A finished box stays picked, with a handle on each side for its width. While typing, drag **Move** on its bar. With the Select tool, click a picked box to type in it. With the Comment tool, click a comment's spot to open it and drag the spot to move it.
- **What each score means**: in Rubric setup, open **What each score means** under a row and write as many of the five levels as you like. While grading, two small lines under that row show the words for the score you picked or are pointing at (a half point shows the two around it), and **All** opens the whole row. Rows with nothing written look as they always did.
- **The rubric bank**: **Save to the bank…** in Rubric setup keeps the rows, what each score means and the grading scale, under a unit you pick (units come from the Planner). The bank list at the top of Rubric setup starts on this assignment’s unit; **Use** fills it in. **Save this scale…** under Grading scale keeps a scale on its own, and **My saved scales** offers the ones with the same total. The Planner’s **Rubrics** tab shows the same bank. Only wording and percents are kept there, never scores or anything about a student. Rubrics saved before the bank come into it the first time Rubric setup opens.
- **Your own rubric**: **Bring in my rubric…** in Rubric setup gives every student their own copy to grade on. A Word rubric stays a Word file: type in it, check its boxes and shade its cells. A PDF or picture comes in as a page to mark up. Anything already saved in that PDF (highlights or comments from Kami, Acrobat or Preview) shows on the page but doesn't count as a mark, so a fresh download isn't shown as marked. A filled-in Total line in a Word file follows a new grading scale, in the file as well as on screen. Replace a Word rubric with another one and Save tells you first: copies you've worked on stay as they are, even under a new file name, everyone else gets the new one, and **Back to the original** on a copy starts that student over on the new one. A marked page stays when a new PDF or picture replaces it. Taking the rubric off, or swapping Word for a page or a page for Word, takes worked-on copies with it, and Save says so first. Scores stay either way. Three gaps: Word copies don't go in the export (see Export marked), a student with no paper keeps the score but loses the shaded copy when the download is reopened, and if the rubric is replaced under a new name on one computer while boxes get shaded on the other, the shaded copy stops showing once the two merge, though the score stays.
- **Past work** (under Next, in the score panel): the student’s scores and your comments from other assignments, beside the new paper, worked out by each assignment’s own rubric. A grade read off the paper counts too, marked “on the paper”, once that download has been opened with this version. Students are matched through the class roster. A name that’s close but not the same, the same name in another class, or a download with no roster is flagged, and its scores stay hidden until you say **Same student** or **Not them**. It never shows during anonymous grading, and it never goes in the Claude ZIP.
- **Consistency check** (Insights tab, Consistency): your scores in the order you gave them, to see whether you got harder or softer as you went. It goes by when each score last changed. With fewer than 9 papers it doesn’t try to say; under 20 it only says maybe; even then it’s only a nudge to look, since later papers may just be weaker or stronger. Rubric rows are read one by one from 20 papers up. Below that, **The same score, side by side** shows every paper with one score next to each other, with its rows and comment. During anonymous grading these show Paper 1, Paper 2, with names covered.
- **Progress, top left**: how many are scored, the class average so far (finished scores only, the same as the Insights tab), how many are left, and how many you left out of the average. Click **Left out** for a list of those papers; click one to open it. A left-out paper with no score still counts as left.
- **Find a student**: type in the box above the list, or press /.
- **All keys**: press ? for the full list.
- **Saved means saved**: the top bar shows "✓ Saved" only once your work has really been stored, and beside it, where. If it can't be stored, a red bar stays at the bottom of the screen until it can, and it keeps trying by itself. "✓ Saved (1 copy)" means your work is safe but the browser's small quick copy is full. With no folder linked, Home reminds you when your last take-home file is more than a week old.
- **A newer copy of a download**: open a new download with a changed or added file and your marks and scores come over per student and per file, your rubric page and Word rubric fill-ins included, if most files match one you've already worked on (if it isn't sure, it asks). Changed and new files get a flag at the top of the file and a ! beside the student's name, and **Looks fine** takes the flag off. The older copy stays on Home, just as it was, along with work on any file or student the new one doesn't have. A student on your roster keeps their score even with no paper in the new one. During anonymous grading the message about it only counts, with no names.
- **An export with a bad file**: the rest still come out, and the export box lists every file that isn't in it and why.
- **Dark mode** is in Settings under Display, and in Find anything (on the Planner too). Papers and slides keep their own light colors.

## Saving to a folder, and taking your work home

These are under **Saving and carrying**, in Settings (the sidebar's "Saved" line goes there too). The app still works from one web address and sends nothing anywhere.

- **Not backed up, until it is**: while your work is only in the browser, the sidebar and the top bar say **Not backed up** in amber, never "Saved" in green, and Home asks once where a copy should be kept. **Not now** puts the question away for a week. In a browser that can't save to a folder (Safari, Firefox), the same card offers the take-home file instead.

- **Choose a folder** (Chrome or Edge): pick a folder on your laptop once. After that every save goes to the browser and to the folder, with no save button. A folder called `Grading Studio` is made inside the one you pick. Choose **Allow on every visit** when Chrome asks, and it won't ask again. If it does ask, the top bar says so and one click reconnects.
- **The top bar says where your work is**: in this browser and your folder, or only in this browser and why. If the folder can't be reached, your work is still in the browser, and the folder catches up by itself when it's back.
- **More than one tab** is safe: a tab left open won't overwrite newer work from another tab. It only saves the students it changed itself, and it picks up the newest work when you come back to it. Remove from this computer, Put back and Bring work in make other open tabs stop and reload. For Put back and Bring work in, those tabs first save what's still being typed there, as a finished comment or text box.
- **Big downloads** (a class of recordings, say) are kept in pieces, so they're kept here, in the take-home file and in the folder like any other. One late file over about 127 MB can't be kept, and the app says so when you add it.
- **If the browser is ever cleared**, link the same folder again and everything comes back: papers, marks, scores, comments, rosters, rubrics, readings, Word edits and added files.
- **Snapshots**: the folder keeps a dated copy each day you work, and one before anything risky: all of the last two weeks, then one a week for three months. Papers stay, since they never change.
- **Go back in time** (Settings): the dated copies by day. **Look** shows how each assignment stood then beside how it stands now. **Restore this assignment** takes one assignment back and leaves everything else alone; a copy of how things are now is made first, and **Undo** is right there. **Restore everything from this copy** takes rosters, units and settings back too. The restore counts as a change made now, so the folder and your other computer follow it.
- **Take my work with me** makes one file protected by a passphrase, holding everything for grading. Planner work isn't in it. If you forget the passphrase, that copy can't be opened by anyone, but your work where you made it isn't affected.
- **Bring work in** on the other computer checks the whole file first, then shows what will change before anything does. A wrong passphrase or a damaged or altered file says so, and changes nothing. The trip back works the same way. A safety copy is made first, so **Put back** undoes it.
- **How merging works**: per student. Each copy knows what it has seen, so the one with changes the other hasn't seen wins, even if the two computers' clocks disagree. A cleared score counts as a change. If the same student changed in both places since they last matched, you see both versions side by side and choose. A download's settings merge one at a time, so a rubric row added here and lighting changed there both stay, and the preview names what comes in. If a rubric row was taken out on one side, scores don't shift: each goes to its own row by the row's name, and the preview says who. If the rows can't be told apart you choose, with each copy's rows shown, and a score on a row with no match comes in blank for you to score again. If both sides changed the rubric setup, the class, anonymous grading, or where the same file went, you choose; moved files, covers, lighting and Claude codes are otherwise pooled, so a reply from Claude matches whichever computer's codes it uses. Put back and your other answers about a scanned page travel too. Opening an assignment card isn't a change to it, and a card changed in both places keeps each change, field by field. If both changed its rubric, you choose. A card's download dropped in again opens on its card, the same as Continue. **If a card's rubric and a download's ever disagree in a way that would change a total or a percent, you're asked which to use**: in the preview when you bring work in, with a question from your linked folder, or when you open the download if the difference was already there (the preview says so). Nothing is picked for you, scores stay saved either way, and from then on the card and the download both use what you picked. Not now opens nothing and asks again next time. With no scores on it, or if only row names or what each score means differ, nothing can change, so nothing is asked and the card's rubric stands. Scores too old to say what rubric they were given with are checked against the last one you saved, and if they don't fit it you're asked. Rosters are never replaced: a name taken off on one side stays off, and if both sides changed, you keep everyone. A forgotten download stays forgotten, and an older file can't bring it back; if the other copy has work from after you forgot it, you're asked.
- **An old backup ZIP** still opens through Bring work in, and now merges the same way. Newer work and your rosters stay. Its work has no history, so the date decides, and the preview says so.
- **Remove from this computer** clears all student work from this browser, safety copies included, once you type REMOVE. It warns you if anything has changed since your last take-home file. It never deletes what's in a linked folder: it says so, stops using the folder, and leaves its files for you. Planner work stays.

Under **About and updates**, in Settings. This is for the web address; the double-click copy stays as it is.

- **It opens with no internet.** After the first visit, the pages are kept on the computer. Your work was always there; now the app is too.
- **Install as an app** gives it its own window and icon. There is no installer: in Chrome or Edge press **Install**, or use the browser's menu, **Cast, save and share**, **Install page as app**. The app and the page in the browser are the same place, with the same saved work.
- **Updates wait for you.** A newer version is fetched quietly and the sidebar says **Update ready**. Nothing changes until you press **Update now**, which saves your work first and reloads once. If you would rather not be asked, choose **Update by itself the next time I open Grading Studio**: even then it never changes while the app is open.

## Audio, video and Word files
- Recordings (mp3, m4a, wav, mp4, mov, webm…) play at the top of each student with 10s skips, 1× to 2× speed, and **+ Time to comment** (adds [2:15] to the ManageBac comment). Keys: Space play/pause, J/L back/ahead 5s.
- **Make transcript** runs speech-to-text in your browser (Whisper, same as the Interview Transcriber; a one-time engine download). Words light up as it plays; click a word to jump there. Transcripts are kept with your marks. Not in the claude.ai preview copy.
- **Comments on Word papers** are real Word comments, so the student sees them in Word's margin. With the Comment tool, click a word (or select some words) and type; **Comment** on the file's bar does the same for selected words, and a right-click on a word works with any tool. Each comment lights its words, has a number after them and a card beside them; click either to change or delete it. Comments already in the file, like a classmate's, show too. A commented Word file goes back in Export marked. New comments are signed "Teacher".
- **Convert to PDF** on a Word paper's bar turns it into PDF pages you can mark, comment on and stamp anywhere, like any other paper: just that file, or every Word paper in the assignment. The pages keep the file's own words (so highlights snap to them and typed names are covered in anonymous grading), its page size, tables, pictures and any comments already in it, numbered and listed at the end. The Word file isn't changed; it waits under Removed files with **Bring back**, and **Undo** is on the note. The converted paper goes back in Export marked once it has a mark. To have this happen as each assignment opens, pick **Convert to PDF pages** under Word papers in Settings. A Word file you've typed in, commented on or taken a grade from is left alone until you convert it yourself, and your own Word rubric always stays a Word file.
- **Numbers and tables in a student's Word file don't set a score.** A grade typed in a student's own Word file is offered only when it's written out as one ("10/12 = 83%", "Score: 10/12"); a date like 10/12 or a page count isn't. A table the student made (numbered rows, "Paragraph 1, 2, 3") is left alone, with a quiet **Score with it** line above the paper if it really is a rubric. A table with levels counting down (4, 3, 2, 1) or labelled as scores still works as a rubric right away.
- Word files (.docx) show as readable pages. Old .doc files can't be read in a browser; a .doc that's really a newer Word file or RTF inside opens anyway.

## Phone photos and files that won't open
- Phone photos open the right way up. A photo you marked before this came in stays as you marked it. Mirrored front-camera photos get turned but stay mirrored.
- iPhone photos (.heic) open as pages, read in your browser by a decoder built into the file, with nothing fetched.
- A picture or Word file with the wrong ending still opens. A file that can't (locked PDF or Word file, damaged file, empty file, Pages, OpenDocument, TIFF, a Google shortcut, a saved web page, and so on) says why and what to do next, with a Download button. You can still give a score.

Each tab has its own address, so Refresh, Back and Forward work. Drag an assignment to another unit, or onto a class in the sidebar; drag a unit's name to move it; and use ↑ ↓ to reorder classes. The small ⓘ beside a name (student list, the paper's heading, Grades list, Students) opens a card with the ID, email (Copy or Email), last year’s level (optional; Class overview then shows the average by level), other spellings of their name, a note, and every assignment in that class with their score or "nothing turned in". Someone with no paper who has a score, comment or status stays in the list when the roster stops naming them, marked **not on roster** in the Grades list, so a renamed student's work is still there to copy over.

## Planner (units and lessons)

Click **Planner** in the sidebar. The Planner is its own page (`planner.html`, or `Planner.html` next to the double-click copy) with its own code and its own saved work. None of the grading code is in it, and it never opens grading work or the grading folder. Your theme choice and your ManageBac links are shared; nothing else is.

The Planner has the same sidebar as grading: **Find anything** (lessons, units, views, the timetable, Dark mode), **Home**, **Planner**, your classes and **Settings**. The classes there are the ones on the Planner’s timetable, with no grading counts, and clicking one opens that class in grading, matched by its name. **Timetable** sits under the classes. The sidebar folds to icons while a lesson, a unit or a document is open. Grading reads the Planner’s timetable and lesson names to show today’s classes on Home; it never writes to them, and nothing of grading’s goes the other way.

- **Calendar.** One tab for the two calendar views, with **Semester | Week** under it.

- **Semester.** Every week of the semester down the side, each course across. Each cell shows the unit, which week of it this is, that week's focus, and how many class meetings already have a lesson. Days off and dates to show are marked. Click a week to plan it.
- **Week.** Each class meeting that week, with the unit strip on top (unit, week number, focus, standards). Click **+ New lesson** on a meeting. Classes with the same course share one deck: a new lesson also goes on the other section's matching class that week.
- **Units.** A unit belongs to a course and runs a number of weeks from a Monday. Each week has a focus, standards and your own notes, plus the class meetings that week. **From Atlas**: paste or drop screenshots of the unit's Atlas page, then **Ask Claude to fill the weeks** (Claude reads the screenshots in the Planner folder) or **Type or paste the weeks** yourself. **Summative and unit files**: task sheets, rubrics and exemplars, kept with the unit. Planning files only, never student work.
- **Rubrics.** Your bank of rubrics and grading scales, sorted by course and unit. Make or edit one here (rows, what each score means, the grading scale), drag it onto another unit, or open it from a unit’s page. Grading’s Rubric setup uses the same bank. With the Planner folder linked, they are copied to `Rubrics/` so Claude can read them or write new ones.
- **Your profile.** One file with your timetable, school calendar, semester dates, units and slide template. Load it once on each computer (**Load my profile…**, or drop it on the page), or put it in your Planner folder as `profile.json` and it loads by itself when it changes. Loading it again never replaces units you already have. Keep it out of this repository: it's yours.
- **Timetable.** Load a setup file (classes, meeting times, days off, dates to show, the semester's first and last week, and a .pptx template), or type it in under Timetable. Nothing about your timetable is built into the app.
- **Slides.** Title, Agenda, Title and text, Plain text and Picture layouts. A new lesson starts with a title slide and one empty slide; new slides start empty, with a gray hint of what goes where. Click text on the slide to edit it. Text size is 28 to 36 pt only. When text runs past the slide you get a button to move the extra lines to a new slide. Speaker notes go under each slide. Drag thumbnails to reorder. **Download .pptx** fills the template from the setup file, so slides keep its masters and layouts.
- **Text tools.** While you type on a slide, the toolbar works like PowerPoint's: **B**, *I* and U, **A ▾** for text color (red, orange, green, blue, purple, gray, or Automatic), **• List** and **1. List** (each turns on or off for the lines you're on), and ⇤ / ⇥ to move a line out or in (Tab and Shift+Tab do the same, up to two levels). Ctrl/⌘+B, I and U work, and Ctrl/⌘+Shift+8 and 7 toggle bullets and numbers. Enter on an empty list line ends the list; Backspace at the start of a line takes its bullet off first. No layout adds bullets or numbers by itself. The .pptx gets real PowerPoint bullets, numbering, indents and bold, italic and underline. Slide text is stored as plain lines: `• ` starts a bullet, `1. ` a numbered line, leading tabs indent, and `**bold**`, `*italic*`, `__underline__` mark words, and `{red}words{/}` colors them (a lesson file carries `"fmt": 2`; older lessons get the bullets and numbers their layout used to add written in, so they look the same).
- **Slides.** **+ Add slide** shows every layout as a picture: title, agenda, objectives, warm-up, exit ticket, directions with steps, discussion question, title and text, two columns, plain text, passage to close read, vocabulary, picture, picture with questions, and video. **Layout** on any slide shows the same pictures and keeps the slide's text. A lesson's **Atlas ↗** opens its unit's Atlas page, or the lesson's own if you give it one. **Look** sets a lesson's colors and background from fourteen simple looks (your own template, Plain White, Soft Cream, Sage, Sky, Blush, Charcoal, Navy Night, Side Stripe, Top Band, Big Type, Framed Paper, Notebook, Chalkboard), and can be your default for new lessons. Text stays between 28 and 36 pt. The .pptx draws the same boxes in the same places on your template, with the look's background in place of the template's.
- **Side panel.** Beside any view: **Books** (your own PDFs, like a routines book or a textbook, kept in this browser only; chapters from the PDF's bookmarks, search, page through, send a page to the Claude box), **Video** (a YouTube link plays beside your lesson and can go on a slide; words open YouTube's search in a tab, because YouTube doesn't allow its search inside other sites), **Atlas** (the lesson's Atlas link, which opens in its own tab because Atlas needs your sign-in, plus the unit's week and Atlas screenshots), **Notebook** (a page of your own thoughts for the open lesson, or the open unit, plus a General notes page: bold, italic, underline, lists, links, YouTube videos that play in the note, and pictures you paste, drop or pick; saved as you type, kept with the lesson so it goes to the Planner folder too, searchable, and one click into the Claude box), and the **Claude box** (drag in slides, chapters and pages, type or dictate what you want, and copy it all at once). Drag the panel's edge to resize it. **🎤 Dictate** uses the browser's voice typing (Chrome or Edge).
- **Materials.** Every lesson has a Materials list under the slide, and every unit has one with its files. Drop in handouts, readings and task sheets, or press **+ New document** to type one on an A4 page: titles, headings, lists, bold, answer boxes, tables and writing lines. It saves as you type, prints, and downloads as a Word file. A Word file you add can be opened with **Edit a copy** to work on it here. **+ Claude box** puts a document's text in the Claude box. Unit documents go to the Planner folder as Word files, so Claude can read them too.
- **Right-click for Claude.** Right-click a slide, a lesson on the week, a file, or any text in the Planner (select words first to quote just those) and pick **Comment for Claude…**. Type or dictate the comment and it goes into the Claude box with what it's about. **Add to the Claude box** adds the thing without a comment. Shift + right-click gives the browser's usual menu, and text boxes keep it so spelling suggestions still work.
- **Search.** The search box in the top bar finds lessons, slide text, notes, documents and units. Press Enter to see the results and click one to open it at that slide.
- **Copy a lesson.** **More ▾ → Copy to another class or day…** (or right-click a lesson) makes a separate copy with its own slides and materials.
- **Print week.** On the Week view, **Print week** prints each day's classes with their lessons, slide titles and materials.
- **Past lessons.** Old decks come in as a lesson pack: one .zip with each deck as a Planner lesson you can edit, plus the original PowerPoint, a PDF and the handouts that went with it. Use **Import…** or drop the .zip on the page. They show on the **Past lessons** tab, grouped by unit and year, and on each unit's page under **Lessons for this unit**; a dated lesson also shows on its day. Click a file on a card to open it. Loading the same pack again skips lessons you already have, and a pack can add a unit's files and fill in its Atlas link. The pack format is described in `src/planner/library.js`.
- **Activities.** A bank of classroom activities (warm-ups, discussions, reading and writing routines, games, checks for understanding), each with a short what-it-is and how-to-run-it. Filter by part of class or kind, then **Add to a lesson** puts in a slide with the steps for students, with the how-to in the speaker notes. Activities marked with a routine number are in *50 Instructional Routines* (Fisher, Brozo, Frey & Ivey, 2015); the wording here is the app's own. **+ My own activity** adds yours.
- **Planner folder (Chrome).** Link a folder just for planning. Lessons are saved there as `.lesson.json` files (pictures in `images/`), units as `Units/*.unit.json` with their files in a folder beside each, your own activities as `activities.json`, plus `timetable.json` and a README for Claude that explains every format. Changes made to those files outside the app (for example by Claude) come back in when you return to the tab or click the folder button. The Planner refuses a folder that holds Grading Studio's grading files.
- **Working with Claude (optional).** Under each slide, **What I want on this slide** tells Claude what you're after, and comments go back and forth on the slide. **Copy for Claude** keeps a version, marks the lesson *With Claude* and copies a message to paste where you're working with Claude. When Claude saves changes into the Planner folder, the lesson says so: **Look slide by slide** shows yours and Claude's side by side to keep either, or **Keep all of it** / **Go back to mine**. **Versions** keeps copies (sent, before Claude's changes, or when you ask) to go back to. You can make lessons entirely yourself and never use any of this.
- **Copy for Claude** without a linked folder copies the lesson as text instead. **More ▸ Save lesson file** and **Import** move a single lesson in and out.

## Good to know
- A new browser starts empty and asks three questions: your classes, how you grade, and where your work is kept. Nothing about any teacher, school or timetable is built in.
- Your marks, scores and comments are kept in this browser, per download. Home lists your downloads newest first, so **Continue** picks up the last one. Drop the same download in again to pick up where you left off.

- Old .doc files can't be opened here (Word .docx files can); they're listed, with what to do next, so you can still give a score.

The Inter font is built into the file (SIL Open Font License; the license is in `src/fonts/OFL.txt` and inside the built file). So is the iPhone photo decoder: libheif and libde265, built by libheif-js, under the LGPL 3. Its license text is inside the built file, and its source is at github.com/strukturag/libheif, github.com/strukturag/libde265 and github.com/catdad-experiments/libheif-js. It sits, packed by build.js, in its own script block (`heicSrc`), so it can be replaced; taking its lines out of build.js leaves a plain explanation for .heic files.
